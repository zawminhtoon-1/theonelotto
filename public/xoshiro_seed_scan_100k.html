📊 vs. the earlier 0–1000 seed range

Seed #168 (best in the 0–1000 range) reached avg 3.0560, a +4.30% lift over baseline. Testing 100x more seeds turns up a slightly better one, #34,945, at avg 3.0880 (+5.38% lift) — an improvement of only 0.0320 avg hits (+1.08 points of lift).

This is exactly what pure sampling noise predicts, not evidence of real predictive power. Each seed's 1000-draw average has an expected standard deviation of ≈0.0363 hits under a null hypothesis of no signal (from hypergeometric variance of matching 21 picks against 6 winners, averaged over 1000 independent draws). Extreme-value theory says the expected maximum deviation across N independently-tested seeds scales with σ·√(2·ln N): for N=1,001 that predicts a max lift of ≈4.61% (observed 4.30%); for N=100,000 it predicts ≈5.95% (observed 5.38%). Both land close to the observed values — casting a 100x wider net catching a slightly bigger "biggest fish" is a textbook multiple-comparisons effect, not a property of seed #34,945 itself. xoshiro256** is a well-mixed PRNG; no seed has real predictive power over future lottery draws.

Best-by-6-hit-count seed #86,023 (26 six-hit draws) again illustrates the same "one metric ≠ another" pattern seen throughout this site's backtests: its avg hits (2.9630) is barely above baseline — it front-loads hits into a handful of jackpot draws while missing more elsewhere, rather than being consistently better.
