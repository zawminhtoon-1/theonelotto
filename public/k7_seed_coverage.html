Draw number vs. hitting seed

Each green dot is one of the 83 covered draws, plotted at (draw #, first-hitting seed) within ±800,000. The 17 red markers on the shelf above the axis are the missed draws — shown for visibility, not at a real seed value.

Pearson correlation (draw # vs. seed, covered draws only): r = -0.144 — no meaningful linear relationship, consistent with the seed formula behaving like a hash rather than a smooth function of draw number. Note the point cloud leans negative (56 negative vs. 27 positive) — that's a scan-order artifact, not a real bias: each draw's "first hitting seed" was found by scanning ascending from −800,000, so negative seeds are systematically found first whenever both a negative and positive seed would have worked.

⚠ Why #2122 stands out

Draw #2122's actual winning combo was never reproduced by any seed across three independently-run ranges (±100,000, ±400,000, ±800,000 — up to 1.6 million seed values checked). Every other draw in the #2024–2123 window was covered by at least one range. This isn't proof #2122 is unreachable at K=7 — just that it sits further out in the seed space than the other 99 draws tested so far.

Coverage = fraction of the 100 draws whose actual 6-number winning combo was a subset of some seed's 7-number pick in the given range.
Methodology: for each seed, generate picks = random.Random(seed×10,000,000+draw_serial).sample(range(1,44),7); a draw is "covered" if any seed's 7-pick contains all 6 actual numbers.
Data captured from a background analysis run in this session (±800,000 sweep took ~14 minutes across 100 draws).
