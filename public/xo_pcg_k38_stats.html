📊 Two-Way K=38 Intersection — xoshiro × PCG64

Current #2134 pool + walk-forward backtest across three windows: #2084–2133 (50 draws), #2033–2133 (101 draws), #1884–2133 (250 draws)

Pool is the intersection of two independently-constructed K=38 pools, both for the same target draw: xoshiro256** seed #692,809 (this site's best-known K=38 xoshiro seed) and PCG64 (O'Neill XSL-RR 128/64) seed #-4,675,555 — the top-ranked seed from the PCG64 K=38 seed scan's Stage 1. No Modular Cycle component here (compare against the three-way xoshiro × Modular Cycle × PCG64 intersection). Both components are pure functions of (seed, draw serial) — no training data needed — and are recomputed live in your browser below, checked against server-embedded references.

The backtest applies this exact construction to three windows — #2084–2133 (50 draws), #2033–2133 (101 draws), and #1884–2133 (250 draws) — re-deriving the pool fresh for every target (no leakage), and tallies hit6b (6 main + bonus), hit6 (6 main, any bonus, equivalent to full containment), hit5, and hit4 against the hypergeometric chance expectation — computed per draw using that draw's actual pool size (33–37 across these windows, not a fixed average), then summed for the significance test.

📌 This is the most consistent above-chance result found on this site so far — worth stating plainly rather than flattening it to "just another null," while still not treating it as proven. hit6b and hit6 both run above chance in all three backtest windows (hit6b: 1.68× / 1.53× / 1.25× for 50/101/250 draws; hit6: 1.46× / 1.48× / 1.29×) — no sign flips between windows, unlike most of the constructions tested on this site, which typically show a result that reverses or collapses once split into sub-windows. Three of the six (window, tier) combinations for hit6b/hit6 individually clear p<0.05: hit6b in the 50-draw window (p=0.0382) and the 101-draw window (p=0.0215), and hit6 in the 101-draw window (p=0.0128) and the 250-draw window (p=0.0214).

Why this doesn't settle it, despite the consistency: (1) the 50-draw window is the only one that's fully out-of-sample (see the in-sample caveat below) — the 101- and 250-draw windows both include a real chunk of draws the PCG64 seed was originally selected against, so their significance is contaminated by that selection bias to some degree. (2) This is one construction among dozens tested across this site's whole investigation, with no formal multiple-comparisons correction applied across all of them — finding one out of many that looks this good is exactly what you'd expect from chance alone at this scale of searching. (3) The pool itself is large (34 of 43 numbers, 78% coverage) — even a real edge this size has limited practical use for elimination. Read this as the most interesting lead this session has produced, not as a validated method — worth watching on future draws, not worth acting on yet.

In-sample seed selection caveat: the PCG64 seed #-4,675,555 was found by scanning against the fixed #1–2050 draw window. This matters more for wider backtest windows, since they reach further back into that same range:

  • 50-draw window (#2084–2133): 0 of 50 draws in-sample — fully clean, out-of-sample throughout. This is the window whose significant hit6b result carries the most weight.
  • 101-draw window (#2033–2133): 18 of 101 draws in-sample (#2033–2050).
  • 250-draw window (#1884–2133): 167 of 250 draws in-sample (#1884–2050) — a majority of this window overlaps the seed's own selection window, so its results deserve the most skepticism of the three.
